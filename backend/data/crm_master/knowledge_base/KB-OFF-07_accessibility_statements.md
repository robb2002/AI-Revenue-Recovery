# Accessibility Statements for Courseware and eTextbooks

Excerpts are short quotations from the cited pages, collected on 2026-11-04. The summaries are our own words.

## Stated target for courseware
Summary (our words): the page states a target level, WCAG 2.2 AA, for courseware and related materials. It is stated as what Pearson builds to, not as an audit result.

> "we build our courseware, learning tools, supplemental materials, and digital textbooks to meet WCAG 2.2 AA." ([source](https://www.pearson.com/en-us/legal-information/accessibility.html))

## Documentation for institutions
Summary (our words): institutions can ask for accessibility documents and the page gives a contact address.

> "We provide accessibility documentation to help schools review our products with confidence." ([source](https://www.pearson.com/en-us/legal-information/accessibility.html))

> "Completed Voluntary Product Accessibility Templates (VPATs) are available for our learning platforms." ([source](https://www.pearson.com/en-us/legal-information/accessibility.html))

> "These documents can be requested by emailing" ([source](https://www.pearson.com/en-us/legal-information/accessibility.html))

The address given on that page is disability.support@pearson.com.

## eTextbook platform
> "Deployed in 2015, Pearson's new eTextbook platform meets most WCAG 2.1 Level AA standards." ([source](https://www.pearson.com/en-us/pearsonplus/legal/accessibility.html))

## Illustrative guidance for procurement questions
- Public-sector tenders often ask for an accessibility conformance report. The page says VPATs are available for learning platforms. Direct the customer to the request address above.
- Do not state that a product "fully conforms" to any standard. The pages say "build to meet" and "meets most".

## Not covered by this document
- Conformance reports for a specific product or version.
- Accessibility of third-party content inside a course.
- Accessibility rules in countries outside those referenced on the cited pages.
