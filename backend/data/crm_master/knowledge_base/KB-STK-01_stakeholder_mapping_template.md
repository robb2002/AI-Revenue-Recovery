# Stakeholder Mapping Template

Illustrative document, SYNTHETIC. It helps a representative record who influences a purchase. Names in a real account come only from stored contacts.

## Purpose
Many deals stall because the decision maker has never been met, or a quiet blocker was never identified.

## Roles to identify
| Role | What they decide | Typical concern |
|---|---|---|
| Sponsor | Wants the change and pushes for it | Outcome for learners |
| Decision maker | Signs or approves the budget | Cost and risk |
| Technical evaluator | Checks integration and security | Fit with systems |
| Academic or programme lead | Judges teaching fit | Quality and workload |
| Procurement | Runs the process | Compliance and terms |
| Legal | Reviews terms | Liability and data |
| End users | Use the product | Ease of use |

## How to complete the map
1. List every contact stored for the account and assign a role.
2. Mark who has been met, who has only been emailed, and who is unknown.
3. Mark each person as supportive, neutral, unknown, or opposed. Do not guess. Use "unknown" until evidence exists.
4. Identify the gap. A deal with no known decision maker is exposed.

## Questions that surface hidden stakeholders
- Who else needs to approve this before it goes to the committee?
- Who has used a similar product before?
- Who will own the project after purchase?
- Is there anyone whose approval would be difficult to obtain?

## Actions for a gap
- Ask the sponsor for an introduction to the decision maker.
- Offer a short briefing for the committee.
- Prepare a one-page summary that the sponsor can forward.

## Escalation
If the decision maker is unknown and the close date is near, raise it at the next deal review.
