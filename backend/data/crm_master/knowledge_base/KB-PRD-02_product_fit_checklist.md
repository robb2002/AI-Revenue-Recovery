# Product Fit Checklist

Illustrative document, SYNTHETIC. It is a working checklist, not a feature specification. Product facts come from the official excerpts (KB-OFF-09, KB-OFF-10) and from the product team.

## When to use it
Use this checklist when a customer says a product "does not do what we need" or asks whether a product supports a specific requirement.

## Step 1: Write the requirement as a sentence
Record who needs it, what they need to do, and why. Example: "Instructors need to reuse last term's course settings." Avoid product names in the requirement.

## Step 2: Check the evidence
1. Is the capability described in an official excerpt in this knowledge base?
2. Is it in a product document that the product team has approved?
3. If neither, the status is unconfirmed. Do not describe it as available.

## Step 3: Classify the answer
| Class | Meaning | What to tell the customer |
|---|---|---|
| Documented | An approved document states it | Share the statement and the source |
| Partly documented | Some parts are stated, others are not | State what is documented and name the gap |
| Not documented | No approved document covers it | Say it is being confirmed, then raise it with Solutions Consulting |
| Known limit | A document states a limit | State the limit plainly |

## Step 4: Check for adjacent needs
Customers often ask for one feature when the real need is another, for example reporting, integration, or accessibility. Ask one follow-up question before answering.

## Step 5: Record the gap
Add unresolved items to the opportunity notes with the date and the owner. If the gap blocks a decision, mark the deal for a follow-up with a named date.

## What not to do
- Do not promise a roadmap item or a delivery date.
- Do not describe a competitor's product.
- Do not combine features from two products as if they were one.

## Escalation
Send unresolved requirements to Solutions Consulting with the requirement sentence, the customer type, and the decision date.
